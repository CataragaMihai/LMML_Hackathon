LMML 2.0: Game Rules 🎲
Welcome to LMML
Welcome to the second edition of the Learning Marathon of Machine Learning! LMML is a 24-hour competition, organised by Sigmoid, where teams solve hands-on machine learning challenges in a CTF-style format. The marathon is a journey around the world: each continent stands for one area of Artificial Intelligence, and each country on the map holds a task waiting for you!
Our goal is for you to learn by doing. You will face real problems, get stuck, get unstuck with the help of our mentors, and leave knowing more than when you arrived. Points and rankings matter, but what you take home matters even more. Please take a few minutes to read these rules before the start; they will save you many questions later. 🙏
Official Game Start
The marathon starts on Saturday, 14 November, at 12:00:00 and ends on Sunday, 15 November, at 11:59:59. Submissions after the end are not accepted, so leave yourself some margin for the last ones.
Before you start
To start solving the moment the map opens, please get these done in advance:
1. Create an account on the platform: https://sigmoid.academy/
2. Read and accept the Terms and Conditions carefully, as they are the rules you agree to play by.
3. Set up your team. The team lead creates it and adds the members, or members send a request to join.
4. Create a GitHub repository shared with your team.
A team has from 1 to 5 members, so you are just as welcome to compete solo. If you would like a team but don't have one, let the organisers know and we will gladly match you with others. Once the marathon starts, teams are fixed, and no one can move from one team to another.
How the map works
When the marathon opens, the world map is yours to explore, and you can take on any available task in any order you like. A correct solution earns points for the whole team and colours the country bright green. A wrong one colours it red, but don't worry, you can always try again. Keep in mind that only your latest submission counts.

	Tasks and submissions
Every task follows the same structure: a short back-story that sets the scene, the objective, the input you receive, the expected output format and the scoring criteria. Read the output format with special care, as a correct answer in the wrong format still counts as wrong.
Depending on the task, you submit a flag (the requested string, in the specified format), a file that is evaluated automatically, or a prompt through a chat-like interface for text-based tasks.
Every task also comes with hints. Each hint costs your team points, so keep them as a last resort. Still, don't be afraid of them: when you are truly stuck, a hint can unblock you and save hours that are better spent on other tasks. 😉
AI Usage Policy
We encourage you to be open about using AI. At LMML, AI is welcome as your adviser, but never as your replacement: we want you to leave able to do things you couldn't do before, and that only happens when the thinking is yours. These rules apply to every participant, on site or remote, and to every task. Claude code may be your main advisor, write code, suggest things, and you are free to adopt this workflows, however the most important part is understanding the task and being able to defend them.
Use AI to understand, to solve but simply fully understand the task at hand. You may use AI to explain a concept, a task description, an error or a library, to discuss possible approaches and their trade-offs, to help you find a bug that you then fix yourself, or to write small snippets you fully understand, such as boilerplate, a plot or a regex. The approach you choose and the core of the solution must be yours. A simple test: could you explain every line to a mentor without the chat open?
No agents working fully in your place. You may not use AI systems that work on a task instead of you without your input: anything that writes your files from scratch, runs code and decides the next step on its own without you does not qualify as your workLML. This includes coding agents and agent modes in IDEs or terminals, multi-agent setups, and scripts that feed AI output straight into your code or the platform. A task committed or pushed by an agent scores zero.
Not sure whether something is allowed? Ask a mentor or the Tech Director before you do it. Asking never costs points!
Proof of performance
Every point you earn should be backed by your own work, so we check it in two ways.
A GitHub link with every solution. Each correct submission must include a link to the commit with that task's working solution, and that commit must be pushed to GitHub within 1 hour of your submission on the platform. Otherwise, the task's points are not counted. We use the push time recorded by GitHub, not the commit date on your computer, so please don't rewrite history on submitted commits with force-pushes or squashing. The linked commit must reproduce your result by following your README. We advise keeping the repository private, but the choice is yours. A tip that makes all of this easy: keep one folder per task, and commit and push as you work rather than only at the end.
Be ready to defend your solution. Throughout the event, mentors will stop by to see how well you understand what you are building. At the end, the mentor teams will interview you about the tasks you solved: why you chose your approach, what a part of the code does, what you tried that didn't work. In a team, every member should be able to explain every solved task. Remote participants are interviewed by video call. During the interview, you will add the evaluators as collaborators on your repository, so they can check your submissions against the linked commits. If you can't show that you solved a task, half of its value is deducted from your total: failing to defend a 200-point task costs you 100 of the points you have accumulated by then. The evaluators' decision is final.

	Mentors
Our mentors are here to help you learn. They won't hand you answers, but they will ask the right questions, explain concepts and point you in a useful direction. They are mostly available during the day; in the evening and at night they can still help, though responses will be slower. 🤗
Event support
All event communication happens on the LMML Discord server. Here is where to turn:
* #announcements: important event updates. Please never mute it.
* #platform-tech-issues: errors or trouble with the platform, such as submitting a task.
* #logistic-support: practical needs, like no free socket or a missed meal.
* #ask-mentors: help when you are stuck on a task (don't forget to ping them). Mentors will reply in the chat or invite you to a huddle, which works especially well for online participants.
* #random: free-topic chat. Inappropriate topics or behaviour lead to a ban.
